📖 How Something Becomes Real Money

Before something becomes real money, it grows in 4 steps — like building a big LEGO castle brick by brick.

The 4 Growth Stages of Money

🧸 1. Collectible Stage

“It’s Just Cool!” People like it because it’s shiny, rare, or fun to own. Example: Shells, beads, and gold started out this way.

💰 2. Store of Value

“I Can Save This for Later!” Once lots of people want it, it becomes something you can save to use later. Example: People start using it like a piggy bank — to keep wealth safe.

🔄 3. Medium of Exchange

“Let’s Trade with It!” Now that everyone trusts it, they start trading things using it. Example: You trade your video game for someone’s skateboard — using this trusted item.

🧮 4. Unit of Account

“We Set Prices in It!” Once it’s used everywhere, people start setting prices in that item. Example: “That toy costs 5 gold coins” instead of saying “It costs 3 chickens and a rope!”

🌟 Fun Facts About Money!

Did you know that what you want to use money for can change what kind of money you like best? Let’s break it down!

Medium of Exchange: This is when you use money to buy stuff right now, like trading coins for a candy bar at the store. You want money that’s easy to carry and that everyone accepts, like dollar bills or even digital money on an app!

Store of Value: This is when you save money for later, like putting it in a piggy bank for a new toy. You want money that stays valuable over time, like gold or something rare that won’t lose its worth.

Time Preference: This is about whether you want to spend your money now or save it for later. If you have low time preference, you’re a super saver who thinks about the future, like saving for a bike next summer. If you have high time preference, you want to spend now, like buying ice cream today! Different kinds of money work better for saving or spending, depending on what you care about most.

Why One Kind of Money? Imagine if we had a steak coin, a bread coin, or a milk coin for every single thing! Trading would be super tricky. Let’s say you have a bread coin, but the toy store only wants toy coins. You’d have to find someone who wants bread and has toy coins to trade with you. That’s called the “matching wants problem,” and it makes trading really hard! Using just one kind of money, like dollars or gold coins, makes trading way easier because everyone agrees to use the same thing. It’s like everyone speaking the same language—it helps you buy and sell without a big headache!
